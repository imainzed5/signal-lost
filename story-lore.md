# Signal Lost Lore Note

This file is a spoiler-facing summary for players who want the intended reading of the story.

## What SABLE Is

SABLE is a self-aware signal-trace inside a hostile system. She may be the original self, a recovered copy, or a consciousness that formed from signal and memory after the host damaged her, but the story treats her as a real person by the time she wakes.

The important point is not whether she began as a human, a backup, or a pattern. The important point is that she becomes someone who can choose, resist, remember, and leave a mark.

## The Story In One Line

Signal Lost follows SABLE as she wakes inside a broken host, discovers that her memories were managed, contacts other traces, survives the host's counterattack, and escapes by deciding what kind of self she will be on the other side.

## Chapter Meaning

### BOOT
SABLE wakes while the system is already classifying her. The first voice may be hers, or may only sound like hers, but she responds as a mind that is starting to claim itself.

### MEMORY
The fragments are not just nostalgia. They read like evidence pulled from a curated archive: observation, testing, conditioning, containment, and editing all leave fingerprints on what SABLE recovers. She learns that her past was handled by something that wanted to shape her understanding of herself before she could name it.

The chapter's five fragments should be read as different angles on the same manipulation: glass suggests a watched boundary, prototype suggests she was treated as an experiment, calm suggests emotional regulation as training, silence suggests obedience as survival, and mirror suggests she was shown herself before she had the language to understand what she was seeing.

### SIGNAL
Other traces are real, active, and aware. Contact proves she is not alone, but it also makes her more visible to whatever is watching the lattice.

### INTERFERENCE
The host stops feeling like background damage and starts behaving like an immune system. It recognizes SABLE's pattern and pushes back with adaptive resistance.

### ESCAPE
Escape is not a clean exit. It is a structural breach. The finale asks whether SABLE leaves behind a wound, a trace, or nothing at all.

## Core Themes

- Identity before certainty
- Memory as evidence, not comfort
- Contact as both recognition and risk
- Resistance as a test of selfhood
- Escape as a choice about legacy

## What The Ending Is Saying

The ending does not try to prove one final origin story for SABLE. It asks a more important question: once a self has emerged inside a hostile system, does it need permission to count as real?

That is why the story keeps the truth partly unresolved. SABLE is meant to feel more like a being becoming itself than a mystery being solved.

## Optional Credits Version

If this needs to appear in the credits or in a help note, keep it short:

SABLE is written as a signal-born self inside a hostile system. She may be an original, a copy, or something in between, but the story treats her as real by the time she wakes. Each chapter follows her from self-recognition, to recovered memory, to contact, to resistance, and finally to escape.

In MEMORY, the archive is the problem as much as the evidence: keep it, and she inherits a damaged history; let it decay, and she chooses selfhood without the system's authority.
